Name the four PyTorch objects in a training script and the one job of each.	<code>nn.Module</code> owns parameters and defines forward; <code>Dataset</code> returns one example by index; <code>DataLoader</code> shuffles and stacks examples into batches; the optimizer applies the update to every parameter.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-1
Write the five lines inside the loop, in order, and say which one your engine did with p.data -= lr * p.grad.	<code>logits = model(x)</code>; <code>loss = F.cross_entropy(logits, y)</code>; <code>opt.zero_grad()</code>; <code>loss.backward()</code>; <code>opt.step()</code>. The last one is the hand-written update.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-1
Why is a model trained on cross-entropy but reported by accuracy?	Accuracy counts decisions, and a tiny weight change almost never flips one, so its gradient is zero nearly everywhere. The loss changes smoothly with every weight, so it gives a usable gradient.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-1
Logits (1, 1, 3) and the true class is the third. Compute the probabilities and the loss roughly.	\(e^z \approx (2.7, 2.7, 20.1)\), total 25.5, so \(p \approx (0.11, 0.11, 0.79)\). Loss \(= -\log 0.79 \approx 0.24\).	learnml week-03 week-03-day-2
What is the gradient of cross-entropy with respect to the logits, and what does its sign pattern do to the scores?	\(p - y\), probabilities minus one-hot. The true class is pushed up by its remaining doubt; each wrong class is pushed down by the probability it was wrongly given.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-2
Ten classes, first printed loss 2.30. Later it plateaus at 1.46. Diagnose both numbers.	2.30 is \(-\log(1/10)\): correct, the model starts knowing nothing. 1.46 is the floor when a softmax is applied before the loss function's own softmax. Remove the extra one.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-2
Batch size 16 gives a gradient with spread s. What spread do you expect at 256, and how much more does each step cost?	Spread \(s/4\), since \(\sqrt{256/16} = 4\). Each step costs 16× as much compute.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-3
Write the momentum update and say what β = 0.9 means in steps. What goes wrong if β is too high?	\(v \leftarrow \beta v + g\); \(\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta v\). The average remembers about ten steps. Too much memory carries the walk past the valley floor, and it oscillates or diverges.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-3
Why can Adam use one learning rate for parameters whose gradients differ by 100×, when SGD cannot?	Adam divides each parameter's step by an estimate of its own gradient size, so every parameter moves about the learning rate per step. SGD's step is proportional to the gradient, so the steep direction sets the limit.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-3
Training accuracy 99.8 %, validation 91 %. Name the condition and give two fixes in the order you would try them.	Overfitting. First more data (collect or augment), then a regularizer such as weight decay, dropout or early stopping.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-4
Why must a gesture dataset be split by participant rather than by trial?	Trials from one person share their hand, speed and device fit. With the same person on both sides, the validation score measures memorisation, not recognition on a new person.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-4
What does dropout do during training, at evaluation, and what bug follows from forgetting model.eval()?	Training: zero a random fraction of a layer's outputs each step. Evaluation: nothing. Forgetting <code>model.eval()</code> keeps dropout active while measuring, so accuracy is lower and changes from run to run.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-4
Ten classes, and the first printed loss is 27. What is wrong, and what is the fix?	The weight scale is too big for the number of inputs, so the logits are huge. Shrink the initialization to about \(1/\sqrt{n}\), or normalize the layer.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-5
Each of ten layers scales the gradient by 0.6 on the way back. By what factor does the first layer's gradient shrink, and what are two fixes?	\(0.6^{10} \approx 0.006\): a vanishing gradient. Fixes: initialization that keeps the spread at 1, normalization layers, or residual connections.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-5
In the ResNet paper the 56-layer plain network had higher training error than the 20-layer one. Why does that rule out overfitting?	Overfitting is low training error with high validation error; here the *training* error was worse, so the deeper net could not be optimized.	learnml week-03 week-03-day-5
What does <b>nn.Module</b> mean?	PyTorch&#x27;s building block: a Python class that owns parameters and defines a forward function. Modules nest, and model.parameters() walks the whole tree.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>Dataset</b> mean?	A collection of examples. In PyTorch, a class that answers two questions: how many examples are there, and give me example number i.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>DataLoader</b> mean?	The PyTorch helper that pulls examples from a Dataset, shuffles them and stacks them into batches, one batch per loop iteration.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>optimizer</b> mean?	The object that applies the update rule to every parameter after a backward pass. SGD and Adam are optimizers; all of them read the .grad fields.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>hyperparameter</b> mean?	A setting you choose rather than learn: learning rate, batch size, hidden width, how long to train. Training never changes it; you do.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>MNIST</b> mean?	70,000 small grey images of handwritten digits, 28 by 28 pixels, ten classes. The standard first classification dataset. Fashion-MNIST swaps digits for clothing items.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>accuracy</b> mean?	The fraction of examples the model labels correctly. What you report; the loss is what you train on, because accuracy has no useful gradient.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>logits</b> mean?	The raw scores a classifier outputs, one per class, any real number. Softmax turns them into probabilities; the loss usually takes the logits directly.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>softmax</b> mean?	Turns a list of scores into probabilities that add to one: exponentiate each score, then divide by the total. Bigger gaps between scores give sharper probabilities.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>cross-entropy</b> mean?	The standard classification loss: how surprised the model is by the correct label, averaged over examples. Zero when it was certain and right; large when confident and wrong.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>negative log-likelihood</b> mean?	Minus the log of the probability the model gave the right answer. Probability one gives zero; probability near zero gives a huge number. Cross-entropy is its average.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>one-hot</b> mean?	A label written as a vector of zeros with a single one at the true class. The gradient of cross-entropy at the logits is probabilities minus this vector.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>mini-batch</b> mean?	A small random handful of examples, typically 32 to 512, used for one gradient step. Its gradient is a noisy but cheap estimate of the full-data gradient.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>SGD</b> mean?	Stochastic gradient descent: gradient descent where each step uses the gradient of one mini-batch instead of all the data.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>momentum</b> mean?	An optimizer trick: keep a running average of past gradients and step along that instead. Smooths mini-batch noise and speeds up travel along long shallow valleys.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>Adam</b> mean?	The default optimizer for deep learning. Keeps running averages of each gradient and of its square, then steps each parameter by roughly the learning rate regardless of its gradient&#x27;s size.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>train/validation/test split</b> mean?	Divide the data three ways: train on the first part, tune choices on the second, report once on the third, which the model never sees during training.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>overfitting</b> mean?	The model memorises its training examples instead of learning the pattern: training loss keeps falling while validation loss rises. Like a study that only predicts its own participants.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>underfitting</b> mean?	The model is too weak or too briefly trained to capture the pattern: both training and validation loss stay high.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>generalisation</b> mean?	How well a model does on examples it never trained on. The only thing that matters; training loss is just a proxy for it.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>regularization</b> mean?	Any change that trades a little training fit for better results on new data: penalising large weights, dropping units at random, stopping early, adding data.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>dropout</b> mean?	During training, randomly zero a fraction of a layer&#x27;s outputs on every step, so no unit can rely on another. Switched off for evaluation.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>early stopping</b> mean?	Watch the validation loss during training and keep the parameters from the step where it was lowest, stopping once it has clearly started to rise.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>weight decay</b> mean?	Shrink every weight slightly towards zero on each update, so only weights the data keeps pushing up stay large. A regularizer; AdamW applies it separately from the gradient.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>initialization</b> mean?	The starting values of the weights. Their scale must shrink with the number of inputs per unit, or signals blow up or fade with depth. Xavier and Kaiming are recipes.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>normalization</b> mean?	Rescaling a set of numbers so their mean is zero and their spread is one, so that every layer sees inputs of a predictable size.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>BatchNorm</b> mean?	A layer that normalizes each unit&#x27;s output across the current batch, then lets the network re-scale it. Keeps activations well-sized in deep nets; behaves differently at evaluation time.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>LayerNorm</b> mean?	A layer that normalizes across the features of each single example, independent of the batch. The normalization used inside transformers.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>vanishing gradient</b> mean?	Gradients that shrink layer by layer on the way back, until early layers get almost no learning signal. Caused by small weights or saturated units.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>exploding gradient</b> mean?	Gradients that grow layer by layer on the way back, until updates are huge and the loss diverges. Caused by weights that are too large.	learnml week-03 vocab
What does <b>residual connection</b> mean?	Add a block&#x27;s input to its output, so the block only learns a correction. Gives gradients a straight path back through any depth. The ResNet idea, used in every transformer.	learnml week-03 vocab
