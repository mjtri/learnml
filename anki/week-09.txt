What does <b>contrastive learning</b> mean?	Training without labels by asking the model to tell matching pairs from non-matching ones: pull true partners together, push everything else in the batch apart.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>positive/negative pair</b> mean?	A positive pair is two things that belong together (an image and its caption). A negative pair is any mismatched combination; in a batch, every other item is a negative.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>InfoNCE</b> mean?	The contrastive loss: for each item, a softmax over its similarities to every candidate in the batch, penalised when the true partner does not win. Cross-entropy, batch as labels.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>CLIP</b> mean?	OpenAI&#x27;s 2021 model: an image encoder and a text encoder trained contrastively on 400 million captioned web images, so a picture and its description land close together.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>shared embedding space</b> mean?	One vector space that two different kinds of input are mapped into, so an image and a sentence can be compared directly by cosine similarity.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>zero-shot classification</b> mean?	Classifying into categories the model was never trained on: write each category as a sentence, embed the sentences, and pick the one most similar to the image.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>prompt template</b> mean?	The sentence a class name is dropped into before embedding, such as &#x27;a photo of a {}&#x27;. Changing the wording changes zero-shot accuracy by several points.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>image–text retrieval</b> mean?	Given a query in one modality, rank items of the other by similarity in the shared space: find the picture for a sentence, or the caption for a picture.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>ImageBind</b> mean?	Meta&#x27;s 2023 model that aligns six modalities (image, text, audio, depth, thermal, motion) by training each one against images only; pairs never seen together still line up.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>ViT</b> mean?	A transformer for images: cut the picture into fixed-size patches, turn each into a token, add position information, and run the same blocks a language model uses.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>patch embedding</b> mean?	The linear layer that turns one flattened image patch (all its pixel values in a row) into a token vector of the model&#x27;s width.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>inductive bias</b> mean?	What an architecture assumes about the data before seeing any: convolutions assume nearby pixels matter; a transformer assumes little and must learn it from data.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>evaluation metric</b> mean?	The number you report to say how good a model is, computed on data it did not train on. Chosen to match what matters, not what is easy to compute.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>precision/recall</b> mean?	Precision: of the items the model flagged, how many were right. Recall: of the truly positive items, how many it caught. Accuracy hides both when classes are unbalanced.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>held-out set</b> mean?	Data kept aside before any training or tuning, touched once at the end. The only number that predicts behaviour on new data. Like the participants your pilot study never saw.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>standard error</b> mean?	How much a mean would wobble if you repeated the whole experiment: the spread of the samples divided by the square root of their number. Shrinks slowly with more runs.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>confidence interval</b> mean?	A range around a measured number that would contain the true value most of the time if the experiment were repeated. Reported as low to high, never one number.	learnml week-09 vocab
What does <b>bootstrap</b> mean?	Estimate uncertainty by resampling your own results with replacement many times and recomputing the statistic each time. The spread of those recomputations is the uncertainty.	learnml week-09 vocab
